\maketitle

The three-layer network's mean-field theory reproduces the exact posterior along the $\kappa_0=0.015$ ray at large $d$,
but at $d=150$ its $L_3$ sits 0.05--0.075 above the sampler's at every $P$, and at large $P$ it misses part of the
second hidden layer. This list tracks what has been tested to find out why. Scripts and runs are in
\texttt{milestones/fcn3\_collapsed\_hmc}; the details behind each number are in \texttt{FINDINGS\_onset.md}, and the
plots are indexed in \texttt{plots/README.md}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cp{0.9\textwidth}@{}}
\mworked & worked: a clear answer that the theory holds, a fix worked, or the suspected cause is confirmed\\
\mfailed & failed: a clear answer the other way: the theory fails, or the suspected cause is ruled out\\
\munclear & indecisive\\
\msetup & done, not a test (runs, solves)\\
\mrunning & running\\
\mtodo & to do
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Setting throughout: $d=150$, $N_1=N_2=1500$, $\chi=1$, $\sigma_A^2=8/1500$, $\sigma_B=1.2554$, $\sigma_w^2=0.5$,
$\epsilon=-0.3\sqrt6$, runs started from the prior, 1000 iterations with 300 of burn-in.

\section*{EK limit: $P\to\infty$ with $\kappa\propto P$}
With $\kappa/P$ fixed every parameter of the theory is unchanged, so only the training set relative to the
population changes. If the gap is a finite-training-set effect it must close.
\begin{itemize}
\worked Theory point $P=3000$ (dataset 2): $L_3$ gap 0.087, 0.030, 0.017 at $P=3000$, 6000, 12000. Closes.
\unclear Theory point $P=24000$ (dataset 0, \texttt{queue\_fixedtheory.sh}): $L_3$ 0.839, 0.874, 0.876 at $P=24000$,
  48000, 96000 against 0.913; gap 0.074, 0.039, 0.037. Halves, then stops; one dataset.
\worked Theory point $P=12000$ (dataset 0): $L_3$ 0.788, 0.817, 0.834 at $P=12000$, 24000, 48000 against 0.855;
  gap 0.068, 0.038, 0.021. Keeps closing.
\failed Hidden layer in these runs (\texttt{plot\_ek\_actions.py}): at theory point $P=12000$ the actions match the
  theory at every $P$ (widths drift slightly above it: sd $h_1$ 0.165, 0.176, 0.182 against 0.168). At theory point
  $P=24000$ the far $\mathrm{He}_1$ group goes 2.0, 4.6, 0 per network, but a large-$h_3$ group ($|h_3|>0.25$, not far
  $\mathrm{He}_1$) appears and grows, 0, 9, 11 per network, like the 17-neuron group at $P=48000$. More data at fixed
  theory does not remove it: the large-$h_3$ group is not a finite-training-set effect.
\todo A second dataset at the theory point $P=24000$, to check that the leftover 0.037 is not one dataset.
\end{itemize}
At the theory point $P=12000$ the gap keeps closing with more data; at $P=24000$ about half of it survives.

\section*{Possible causes}

\subsection*{The finite training set}
The theory replaces the training loss by its population average. Explains the first half of the gap.
\begin{itemize}
\worked The networks overfit: test MSE / training MSE 2.5--3.5 on the $P$ scan; the $\mathrm{He}_3$ residual on the
  training set is about half the population residual (\texttt{overfit\_check.py}).
\worked Each hidden neuron is pulled by the training residual: its exact pull is 92--99\% correlated with the theory's
  form but 0.44--0.93 of the size the population residual implies (\texttt{hidden\_equation\_test2.py}).
\worked Along the ray the overfitting closes with $d$ as the $L_3$ gap does: test/train 2.75, 1.67, 1.39 at $d=150$,
  300, 600 (\texttt{ray\_overfit\_check.py}).
\worked The overfitting is in the learned features, not the readout: a readout on fixed features has at most 1500
  degrees of freedom, too few to give a 2$\times$ residual ratio at these $P$.
\worked Where: an ordinary hidden neuron's dependence on the perpendicular directions correlates with the training
  residual (22--29 nats of fit gain on the training set, 1--2 on fresh data) (\texttt{outer\_he3\_fresh.py}). At
  $P=750$ and 1500 the hidden layer also picks out one training-set direction, $\sum_\mu y_\mu x_{\perp,\mu}$
  (overlap 0.4--0.66), which is zero in the population; gone by $P=6000$ (\texttt{plot\_perp\_projections.py}).
\worked The mass between the read-in wells (the flat shelf at 6--8 nats in the read-in action plots; 0.6--4.6 neurons
  per network against 0.002--0.004 in the theory). It is made of brief inward excursions of outer neurons, neurons
  crossing while the count drifts ($P=6000$), and 1--3 neurons per network that sit in the middle of the barrier for
  most of the run with large $q\approx0.6$--0.75. Those neurons' perpendicular weights point along the training set's
  residual direction $\sum_\mu r_\mu x_{\perp,\mu}$ ($|\cos|$ 0.16--0.24, and 0.10--0.12 with $\sum_\mu y_\mu x_{\perp,\mu}$)
  against 0.065 for inner neurons and for random directions, at $P=1500$--6000. The theory's read-in action depends on
  $w_\perp$ only through $q$, so it cannot hold a neuron there. With the theory fixed and $P$ doubled twice the barrier
  empties: 1.3--1.7, 0, 0.02 neurons per network at theory point $P=3000$ (dataset 2); fractions 0.0009, 0.0001, 0 at
  theory point 12000. The neurons choose a side, but not in the theory's proportion: the outer count goes 26--27, 37, 39
  at theory point 3000 (theory 28) and 47, 55, 60 at theory point 24000 (theory 108).
\todo Theory averaged over random training sets of size $P$ instead of the population.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Finite width, $N=1500$}
Candidate for the half of the gap that survives more data.
\begin{itemize}
\unclear The $\mathrm{He}_3$ learning sits on few neurons: 50--120 outer read-in neurons, 17 large-$h_3$ hidden
  neurons, 1--3 far $\mathrm{He}_1$ neurons. The theory treats each as a smooth density. The read-in $\mathrm{He}_3$
  kernel then fluctuates by about $1/\sqrt{55}\approx13\%$, and (E2) changes shape for 10--20\% changes in its input
  (\texttt{hidden\_equation\_test.py}).
\unclear The 17-neuron group at $P=48000$: all of their $h_3$ comes from the $\sim$55 outer read-in neurons; fit gain
  470--535 nats each against the theory's 225--250 at the same neurons and its prior cost 420--460. Half of their
  pull comes through the perpendicular directions, from the network's own mean output, and is as large on fresh
  data, so not overfitting. The theory treats each hidden neuron's perpendicular dependence as independent noise;
  with $\sim$55 shared read-in neurons it is not (\texttt{outer\_he3\_group.py}, \texttt{outer\_he3\_fresh.py}).
\unclear Earlier width checks: on the ray at $d=600$ doubling $N$ (with $\chi=2$, theory unchanged) raises $L_3$ from
  0.694 to 0.705--0.707 and halves the gap; at $d=150$, $P=3000$ it changes $L_3$ by less than 0.001, but those
  $N=3000$ runs stopped at iterations 415 and 355 of 1000 (115 and 55 after burn-in). Not tested at $d=300$, nor at
  strong learnability at $d=150$.
\unclear The perpendicular part of the network's mean output, which the theory sets to zero (mean square of the
  output minus its best fit by functions of $x_0$, on fresh inputs; \texttt{notes/perpendicular\_term\_fcn2\_fcn3.tex}):
  on the ray at $d=600$, $P=48000$, same dataset, 0.0148--0.0154 at $N=1500$ and 0.0110--0.0111 at $N=3000$
  ($\chi=2$). It shrinks with width, but by 27\%, not by half; it is also larger at small $P$ (0.028 at $d=150$,
  $P=3000$; 0.009 at $d=150$, $P=48000$), so part of it looks like the finite training set.
\todo $N_1=N_2=3000$, $\chi=2$ at $P=24000$, $\kappa=0.015$ and at $P=48000$, $\kappa=0.03$. Finite width if the
  leftover gap and the 17-neuron group shrink.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{The sampler's read-in count is not in equilibrium}
Not a theory error, but it would look like one.
\begin{itemize}
\worked The number of outer read-in neurons is not at equilibrium from $P=6000$ on. Up to $P=3000$ it settles at the
  theory's within 25 iterations. At $P=6000$ and 12000 it starts above the theory's, falls through it and is still
  falling at the last iteration (12000: from about 90 to 64--70, theory 78). At $P=24000$ it starts at about half the
  theory's and creeps up (42--47 to 49--57, theory 108); at $P=48000$ it is fixed (54--63, theory 150). In the
  $\kappa\propto P$ runs: 55 and 60 at theory point $P=24000$, 96 and 114 at theory point $P=12000$. The far $\mathrm{He}_1$
  group and the 17-neuron group are also the same neurons for the whole run. The inner $h|\mathrm{He}_3$ double well
  is in equilibrium (its neurons switch sign freely).
\unclear Read-in kernels from the saved networks are 10--12\% weaker than the theory's at $P=3000$--12000 and 26--38\%
  weaker at 24000--48000 (\texttt{empirical\_kernel\_test.py}).
\failed Theory with the outer read-in count held at each run's value (read-in prior tilted by
  $e^{\mu\,1[|w_0|>\text{barrier}]}$, $\mu$ scanned) (\texttt{theory\_fixed\_count.py}). The count is stiff in the theory:
  20 nats per neuron moves it by only 15--25\%, the outer well moving out to compensate for fewer neurons. Held at
  the runs' counts the theory's $L_3$ drops by at most 0.009, so the gaps stay: 0.035--0.08 on the $P$ scan,
  0.027--0.042 for the $\kappa\propto P$ runs (at theory point $P=24000$: 0.030 and 0.029). The frozen count is not
  the cause of the gap.
\failed The hidden layer of the theory held at the runs' counts (\texttt{theory\_fixed\_count\_hidden.py}; $n=49$ at
  $P=24000$, $n=40$ at $P=48000$, last solves not fully converged). Holding the count low moves the theory away from
  the sampler: the far $h|\mathrm{He}_1$ wells of the free theory at $P=48000$ disappear, and $h|\mathrm{He}_3$ gets
  narrower (action at $|h_3|=0.4$: 129 and 174 nats, against 95 free). The frozen count does not produce the
  sampler's hidden-layer groups.
\todo Sampler started with the theory's count.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Ruled out}
\begin{itemize}
\failed A higher $x_0$ Hermite tower: with the tower at $\mathrm{He}_{11}$, 21, 31 or 41 the perpendicular share of the
  17 neurons' pull stays 0.48--0.57 (\texttt{outer\_he3\_tower.py}).
\failed The term the theory drops from the output-dual covariance, $\chi(\sigma_A^2K+\kappa/P)^{-1}$: 0.02--0.18 nats per
  hidden neuron, flat in $h$ (\texttt{cavity\_decomposition.py}).
\failed The factor $1/(1-\ell)$ the theory drops from each hidden neuron's readout term, where $1-\ell$ is the posterior variance of that neuron's readout weight over its prior variance ($\ell=0.04$--0.31 from $P=750$ to 48000): raises $L_3$ (away from the sampler) and
  does not split $h|\mathrm{He}_3$ (\texttt{onsager\_test.py}).
\failed A $\sigma_B^2$ mismatch: handled identically in theory and sampler.
\failed Further theory wells in the hidden layer beyond the plotted range: none out to $|h_1|=8$, $|h_3|=3$.
\worked Fixed along the way, not causes: the theory's local maximum at $P=750$; the theory's hidden-layer marginals
  (the prior covariance is nearly singular; now computed along prior regression lines, matching the solver's moments
  to 1--2\%).
\unclear The theory's read-in barrier height is reproducible only to about 15\%: two solves of the same point
  ($d=150$, $P=3000$) agree in $L_3$ (0.61682), the wells and the flanks up to $\sim$20 nats to 0.1--0.4 nats, but put
  the barrier top at 38.9 and 32.6 nats. The barrier holds no mass, and it is set by near-singular directions of the
  hidden prior covariance. Comparisons of barrier depth with the samplers (which resolve only $\sim$9 nats) are
  unaffected; quoted theory barrier heights carry this uncertainty.
\unclear Not testable alone: (E2) fed measured inputs open loop is too sensitive to its input to judge
  (\texttt{hidden\_equation\_test.py}, \texttt{hidden\_equation\_test2.py}); the hidden-layer solve on the sampler's
  read-in kernels is not self-consistent at $P=12000$, 24000 (\texttt{empirical\_kernel\_test.py}).
\end{itemize}
